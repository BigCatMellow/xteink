## 2. Historical or political text

**Historical setting:** Three to six dates/events relevant to the author, the publication, and the people discussed.

**Provenance:** What is the author's relationship to the events? Is this primary testimony, political advocacy, edited autobiography, or later research?

**Interpretive claim:** What event or mechanism does the text say matters?

**Different interpretation:** Reconstruct another serious account accurately and explain whether evidence favors either one. Don't confuse “two sides exist” with “both have equal support.”

**Deliverable:** 500–750 words with a claim, source-based evidence, one substantial objection, and a revised conclusion.

**My diagnostic:** Attempt an introductory question without notes. Record missing steps, not just the score.

**Model and definitions:** State variables, units, assumptions, and what would count as evidence.

**Diagram or reproducible chart:** Include denominators, labels, sources, and uncertainty.

**Scientific claim:** What finding would contradict the proposed mechanism? What confounders or selection effects matter?

**Reattempt:** Solve two previously missed problems after a few days without consulting the original answers.

**Advance only when ready:** Aim to solve most representative problems independently. An 80% target is a suggested self-check, not a university grading rule.

## 5. Module capstone

**Question:** Choose a specific, answerable question linking at least two disciplines (e.g. how a political institution changes incentives; how a literary voice alters historical memory; what makes an AI claim credible).

**Sources:** At least one primary text and one independent scholarly or empirical source, when appropriate. Keep a bibliography with editions, translation, author, publication, and URLs/pages.

**Method:** Explain whether your task uses close reading, historical corroboration, quantitative analysis, modeling, or a combination.

**Original work:** 1,000–1,500 words **or** a reproducible data/code project with a concise written explanation. This is a suggested study target, not mandatory homework.

**Review rubric (self-assessment: 0–3 each):**

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accurate understanding | No defensible account | Mostly plot/claims | Accurate with some nuance | Accurate plus limits and context |
| Evidence | Unsupported | One weak example | Two appropriate sources | Independently checked, appropriate evidence |
| Reasoning | Conclusion only | Some reasoning | Clear argument and objection | Persuasive reconstruction and revision |
| Original synthesis | None | Loose summary | Reasoned connection | Novel testable/defensible insight |
| Method and uncertainty | None | Vague caveat | Appropriate method noted | Assumptions, alternatives, and limits transparent |

**Revisit in four to eight weeks:** What would you now revise? Which prerequisite skill is still weak? What should you read or practice next?
